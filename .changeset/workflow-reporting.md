Reporting is built in on Postgres: `PostgresJourneyStore.reporting` records where each instance stands and how it ended, every message copy, and the opens and clicks on it, and `journeyWorker` installs it as the default observer. With `WORKFLOW_ORIGIN` set, journey emails' links and open pixel are tracked through the Worker's own `/t/...` routes: every open and click is classified as a person's or a machine's — strong signals alone (scanner user agents, cloud and security-vendor networks, Apple Mail Privacy Protection, HEAD requests, three or more links or every link within seconds, which reclassifies the burst's earlier clicks too), weak ones only together (a click within `emails.tracking.quickClickMs` of delivery, 1 s by default, plus a second link within seconds), with the request's network (ASN, organization, country, never the address) stored beside each verdict — clicks redirect only to the recorded URL, and the config's `emails.unsubscribe` link asks for confirmation before it records the unsubscribe. A push carries its `message_id`; `JourneyClient.messageOpened(userId, messageId)` (`POST /messages/opened`) records the tap. `sweepSegments` (`journeyWorker(...).sweep`, `scheduled`, `POST /segments/sweep`) keeps every deployed segment's membership, starts the journeys of segments entered by the clock alone and snapshots each segment's size; it replaces `evaluateSegmentTriggers`. A segment trigger starts journeys for users who enter after it is deployed.
