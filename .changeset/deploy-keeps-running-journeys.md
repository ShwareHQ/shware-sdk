A deploy now refuses to break the journeys still running. A journey is pinned to the workflow IR it entered on, but the IR names its templates, segments and actions by id and resolves them when it reaches them — on purpose: the latest content goes out and a segment is a live audience, as in Customer.io, Braze and Iterable (README "Versioning" lays the comparison out, with sources). The one thing a deploy must not do is take away what a pinned IR still names. `deployBundle` now holds the bundle, and with a `config` the templates the code would serve, against the pinned IRs (`checkInFlight`, `pinnedWorkflows`): a segment or action dropped, a template gone or moved to another channel, a new required prop the pinned props never supplied, and refuses with `InFlightError` — `409` on `POST /deploy` and `POST /bundle/deploy`, listing each pinned version, its running journeys and what breaks. A compatible change (a reworded template, a new optional prop, a changed segment condition) passes; `?force=1` (`breakInFlight`) deploys anyway and reports what it broke in `DeployResult.inFlight`. `journeyWorker` runs the check when a version applies its own bundle and leaves the routes as they were on a refusal, logged once. Breaking: `JourneyStore` gains `runningPins()`, `deployBundle` takes an options object (`{ version, force, config, breakInFlight }`) in place of the positional `version, force`, and `DeployResult` gains `inFlight`. The IR header's "per-hash segment snapshots are future work" is replaced by this design.
